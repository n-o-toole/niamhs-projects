## Hi there 👋
I have just completed my MSc in Space Science and Technology and I am currently doing an internship in Quantum Communications. I am sharing the various programming projects I have completed over the past few years here. 

I initially learned Python during my BSc in Physics with Astrophysics at University of Galway. I completed many Python projects and assignments, most notably of these was my final year project. The aim of the project was to use the Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer (WISE) to identify exceptional planet forming disks through data and image analysis in Python. I created a processing pipeline to query, clean, and analyse catalogue data, and I performed image processing and photometric analysis on 52 multi-band infrared images. I extracted physical features from imagery to identify and predict ideal targets for future observations for planet formation research. My Jupyter notebooks from this project can be found in the BSc Final Year Project above, along with my thesis.

I significantly improved my Python programming skills during my Master's where Python was an integral part of the course. I expanded my skills through projects and assignments across various subjects. This allowed me to develop skills in data analysis and visualisation, earth observation analysis, curve fitting, modelling, and satellite subsystem development. The Earth Observation Analysis folder above features a Jupyter notebook (SENTINEL 2.ipynb) where I worked on analysing and extracting features from Sentinel-2 images. The images featured different islands and the work included converting pixel distances to actual distances to measure land area and using thresholds, removing noise, and extracting contours to create a masked image of the island to isolate it from the background. The folder also features a Python script (earth_observations.py) that was used to analyse images of a forest that experienced a wildfire. The Normalised Burn Ratio (NBR) was measured for each pixel in the images and this was plotted to show the changes over time as the forest was burned and the vegetation changed. Similarly, images of a lake were analysed to assess water quality over time. The Normalized Difference Turbidity Index (NDTI) was measured and plotted to illustrate this. The plots produced are included in the folder.

During my Master's I worked on a project with 4 of my classmates to design, build, test, and launch a CubeSat satellite. Our project was RadSat; a compact satellite for atmospheric radiation monitoring. The satellite flew on a high-altitude balloon to 23 km above ground and aimed to measure the radiation profile of
the atmosphere. My main role on the project was software development; I programmed 4 MicroPython scripts to operate the satellite, communicate with the ground station, measure temperature and pressure, determine GPS coordinates, obtain radiation measurements, and store all data onboard. This allowed me to significantly improve my programming skills while also strengthening my problem solving, team work and communication skills as there was many issues encountered along the way that we had to solve as a team under time pressure. The RadSat folder above features the scripts that I developed during the project.

The Python Projects folder above features some extra examples of work I have done in Python. The High energy detectors Notebook features work I did during my Master's calibrating radiation detectors in the lab. This involved setting up the detectors with radiation samples, measuring radiation levels, and analysing the data collected. The analysis mainly involved curve fitting and modelling to determine the relationship between the energy and the detector channels using multiple sources and different detector types. The folder also shows a Python script that was used to create a Hertzsprung-Russell diagram with Hubble observations of the globular cluster NGC 1261 in two filters. The script contains functions for cosmic ray removal, star-finding, photometry, and plotting the HR diagram.

I am currently completing my internship at Walton Institute where I am working in research and experimental work for quantum communication. I am involved with many ongoing projects, such as designing an optical ground station (OGS) that will be built in Waterford for free-space optical communication. One application for the ground station with be performing quantum key distribution (QKD) with the EAGLE-1 satellite being developed by ESA. I am also working on a lab-based experiment to demonstrate the performance of a pointing, acquisition, and tracking (PAT) system that may be used in the OGS. Additionally, I am doing some data analytics and visualisation work for a proposed weather station. I am working on ideas for a dashboard that will display weather conditions, provide live updates, and predict future conditions. My first prototype for this was developed in Grafana, and that can be seen in the Grafana folder above. 

I will continue to share my projects here in the future. Thanks for visiting my page and if you have any questions feel free to contact me!😁
